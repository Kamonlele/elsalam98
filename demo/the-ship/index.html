The ship

A ferry of the “Poets” class

The Boccaccio was a 130 metre ferry built between 1969 and 1970 by Italcantieri, later Fincantieri, in Castellammare di Stabia, for the state company Tirrenia. With her four sisters Carducci, Manzoni, Pascoli and Petrarca she sailed mainly between the Italian mainland and Sardinia until 1999.

1990 to 1991: before and after

Taller, heavier, less stable

In 1991 the Boccaccio and her sisters were rebuilt to carry more passengers: four upper decks were added, with sponsons along the hull.

As the official drawings in the lawsuit show, she left the shipyard as a new ship: top heavy, wider, taller, more exposed to the wind, with a completely different and poorer stability. According to the families’ technical experts, that transformation is at the root of the tragedy.

Sold to Egypt in 1999

In April 1999 Tirrenia sold the ship for about 2 million dollars to Pacific Sunlight Marine Inc. of Panama, a company of the Egyptian Ismail family. Renamed Al Salam Boccaccio 98, she sailed the Red Sea and the Mediterranean under the flag of Panama.

The other transformed sisters were bought by the same group. From late 2005 until the sinking the ship was managed by El Salam Maritime Transport Co., at the same Cairo address as Pacific Sunlight Marine and El Salam Shipping & Trading: 30 Yakoub Artin, Heliopolis.

The end of the sister ships

Scrapped within months

Several hypotheses have been raised about the stability of the ferry. The added decks, even with the sponsons, may have seriously compromised her balance, reduced the freeboard and the stability margins.

What is certain is that between June 2006 and January 2007 all the surviving sisters, Al Salam Pascoli 96, Al Salam Manzoni 94 and Al Salam Carducci 92, were withdrawn from passenger service and scrapped in Alang, India. Is it a coincidence? We do not believe so.
